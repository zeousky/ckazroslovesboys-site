Shrub edges

Northern Cardinal

Cardinalis cardinalis

The pointed crest and heavy orange bill are the quickest clues. Males are scarlet with a black face. Females have a warm brown body and red on the wings, tail and crest.

Listen for clear, slurred whistles from the top of a hedge.

Hear the northern cardinal at Audubon

Lawns

American Robin

Turdus migratorius

Look for a rusty chest, gray back and yellow bill. On short grass a robin often runs a few steps, stops upright and tips its head before probing.

Check fruiting trees as well as the grass, especially in colder months.

Hear the American Robin at Audubon

Oaks and woodland edges

Blue Jay

Cyanocitta cristata

A blue crest, white face and dark collar make a bold pattern. A jay in flight shows bright white patches on the wings and tail.

Listen near oaks. The harsh jay call is often easier to notice than the bird.

Hear the blue jay at Audubon

Open ground and wires

Mourning Dove

Zenaida macroura

Look for a small head, a slender neck and a long, pointed tail. The body is soft gray-brown, with a few dark spots on the folded wings.

A dove taking off often makes a whistling sound with its wings.

Hear the mourning dove at Audubon

Tree trunks and small branches

Downy Woodpecker

Dryobates pubescens

Check for a white back, spotted black wings and a very short bill. Males have a red patch at the back of the head.

A similar Hairy Woodpecker has a much longer bill. Compare the bill with the length of the head.

Hear the downy woodpecker at Audubon

Branch tips and feeders

Black-capped Chickadee

Poecile atricapillus

A little round bird with a black cap and bib, broad white cheeks and buff sides. It can cling upside down while searching a twig.

The Carolina Chickadee looks similar where the ranges meet. Use local range and voice together.

Hear the black-capped chickadee at Audubon
